# Part 4 Summary — The Shock Is in the Filing: Disclosure Moves, Self-Description Barely Does

## Question

When an external shock hits, which channel moves first: the formal
disclosure document, or the self-description a firm can edit any
day? And does the aggregate answer hold firm by firm?

Diversity-inclusion language is the test case: a dated shock (2020),
a dated reversal (2023), measured on both channels with the
identical instrument (dictionary theme shares; About side restricted
to about-type, non-sparse documents). My initial hypothesis, from
the aggregate curves, was a lead-lag: disclosure peaks in 2021,
websites follow in 2022. This part reports what happened when I
tested that hypothesis properly — it did not survive, and what
replaced it is more interesting.

## Findings

**1. The disclosure channel moved a lot.** The proxy
diversity-inclusion share rose from 0.084 (2016) to a peak of 0.160
in 2021, the statements reporting fiscal 2020, nearly doubling,
then receded to 0.119 by 2024. This curve rests on complete
coverage (450 of 450 documents), so it is not a composition
artefact.

**2. The advertising channel appeared to follow — in aggregate.**
The mean About-page share rises to a peak of 0.092 in 2022 and
recedes to 0.056 by 2024, seemingly a year behind the proxy.

**3. The paired check overturns the website "surge".** Restricting
to the 12 firms present in the sample in both 2021 and 2022, the
mean About share moves from 0.071 to 0.079 (+0.008; 2 firms up, 8
flat, 2 down) — against +0.026 unpaired. The 2022 aggregate spike
is mostly sample composition: firms whose pages became substantive
enough to measure in 2022 (Starbucks, whose page grew much richer
that year, is the clearest case). For continuously observed firms,
About-page diversity language has **no clear peak year**: it drifts
up gently from 2020 to 2022 (paired: 0.058 → 0.077) and back down
by 2024 (0.060). There is no one-year lag to explain, because there
is no sharp website movement to explain.

**4. Firm-level peak timing agrees.** Among firms with at least six
usable years on both channels, peak years split evenly (4
proxy-first, 4 About-first; 7–7 in a wider cut) — no typical
sequence exists.

## The finding

The shock is visible in what firms file, not in what they
advertise. Proxy DEI language nearly doubled (0.084 → 0.160) and
then receded; for firms observed continuously, About-page DEI
language barely moved. The apparent 2022 website surge is mostly
sample composition: firms whose pages became substantive enough to
measure. Once the pressure came off, both channels receded
substantially by 2024 (proxy -26%, About -39% from peak) — stated
without ranking the two, which these samples cannot support.

In other words, the "say" channel is far stickier and less
shock-sensitive than the "do" (disclosure) channel. This is
consistent with the rest of the project: Part 1 found About pages
change substantively in only about half of company-years, and
Part 3's index derives most of its year-to-year movement from the
proxy side. One plausible explanation — offered as such, not
tested here — is institutional: the proxy is produced on a forced
annual calendar, under legal review, in direct response to the
fiscal year just ended and to shareholder proposals on the ballot;
the About page has no calendar, no reviewer, and no forcing event,
so it changes rarely and idiosyncratically.

The exceptions are instructive rather than contradictory. Merck
did make one large website move — writing diversity into its core
self-description in 2022 ("We foster a diverse and inclusive
global workforce and operate responsibly every day...") — and Meta
was already at a high level in 2021. The website channel *can*
move; it just usually doesn't, and when it does, it is one firm's
decision, not a wave.

## Limitations and what I would do with more time

- **The proxy's rise may itself be partly compelled.** From around
  2020-21, institutional investors and proxy advisers such as ISS
  began pressing for board-diversity disclosure, and Nasdaq adopted
  a comply-or-explain board-diversity disclosure rule (approved by
  the SEC in 2021). Some of the proxy channel's near-doubling may
  therefore reflect external disclosure requirements rather than
  firms' own changing priorities — the "did" side of this project
  is itself a text produced under pressure, not observed behaviour
  (Part 3, limitation 1). The finding is about which *channel*
  responds to shocks, mandates included, not about which firms
  became more committed.

- The paired About sample is small (12 firms for 2021–22) because
  continuously measurable About pages are rare; the flat paired
  result could hide small movements in either direction. It cannot,
  however, hide a movement anything like the proxy's doubling.
- Peak-year and share-based tests are coarse. With more time:
  monthly Wayback captures for an event study around mid-2020, and
  the same paired design applied to sustainability, whose proxy
  crest (a 2022–23 plateau at ~0.26) is later and larger — does the website
  channel ignore that shock too?
- Composition effects deserve the same suspicion everywhere in this
  project; I report paired cuts alongside aggregates throughout
  for exactly this reason.

Anyone tracking corporate response to a social shock should watch disclosures, not websites, and should check firm-level patterns before trusting an average.
