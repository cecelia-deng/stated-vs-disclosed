# Part 1 Summary — What Companies Say About Themselves

Every large company maintains a page that answers the question "who
are we?" — its About page. This part of the project treats those pages
as data. Using the Wayback Machine's web archive, I set out to
recover the About page of 50 major US companies for each year from
2016 to 2024,
and asked three questions: What values do companies claim? How often
do they rewrite those claims? And how have the claims drifted over
nine years?

**What companies claim.** I coded every page against nine value
themes: innovation, customer focus, integrity, sustainability,
diversity and inclusion, employee wellbeing, shareholder value,
community, and excellence. I used two independent methods (an AI
language model and a keyword dictionary) that agree with each other
53–90% of the time depending on the theme. (Figures below are for
genuine About pages; for about half the sample the closest recoverable
equivalent in some years is the homepage, which reads more like
marketing and is reported separately.) The most universal claims are
customer focus (87% of About pages), innovation (80%) and excellence
(80%). The least universal is employee wellbeing (20%). That
figure comes with a measurement caveat: wellbeing is often phrased generically ("our
people", "our team"), and theme coding may undercount it.

**How often the story changes.** Comparing each About page with the
same company's previous available year, about half (51%) show a
substantive rewrite of the stated values. Rewriting peaked in 2020,
the pandemic and protest year, in 65% of year-to-year
comparisons; other years sit between 42% and 56%. Some
companies almost never touch their page (Chevron, Morgan Stanley,
Starbucks and Wells Fargo rewrote in only 2 of 8 year-to-year
comparisons); others, such as Amazon and Intel, rewrite nearly every
year. Homepages, by contrast, change 75% of the time, mostly for
product and news reasons, which is why the two page types are never
pooled here.

**The diversity arc.** The clearest nine-year story is diversity and
inclusion language. On genuine About pages it was present in 40%
of pages in 2016, stayed in a 35–42% band through 2021, and reached
53% in 2022 before falling to 37% by 2024. The six companies with
an About page in all nine years point the same way, but are too few
to be conclusive — and Part 4 shows that much of the 2022 jump
reflects which pages become measurable that year rather than
companies changing their language.

**Honest caveats.** Archived pages are not a perfect mirror. Some
companies' About pages could not be recovered for some years, and for
about half the sample the closest equivalent is the homepage, which is
more marketing than mission statement. I documented every gap rather
than papering over it, flagged the cases where an apparent "change"
was really a website migration (Meta's move across three domains), and
checked that my two coding methods tell roughly the same story before
believing either. The picture that survives those checks is the one
above: a common vocabulary of customer, innovation and excellence —
and diversity language whose apparent 2022 surge turns out, on
closer inspection (Part 4), to be mostly a change in which pages I
can see.

If you want to know what a company values now, its About page is a lagging and sticky signal; read it alongside its filings.
